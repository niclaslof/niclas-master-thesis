\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

In the realm of automated music analysis, the task of music instrument recognition stands as a pivotal challenge. As digital music libraries expand, the need for optimal features---those that can succinctly and effectively represent musical nuances---becomes paramount. This thesis delves into this challenge by juxtaposing two distinct paradigms of feature extraction: the meticulously designed Scattering Transform and the learned penultimate features from MusicNN models (MSD and MTT). The primary challenge addressed is the comparative performance of designed versus learned features in music instrument recognition, a problem of paramount importance given its implications for the broader field of music informatics.

The significance of this exploration is underscored by the direct impact of feature choice on the accuracy and efficiency of recognition systems. With the burgeoning size of digital music libraries, effective recognition can revolutionize tasks like music recommendation, cataloging, and analysis. The inherent complexity of musical compositions and the diverse nature of musical instruments have made this a persistent challenge. Leveraging the ESSID dataset and a custom-curated dataset of 70 tracks from YouTube, classifiers such as KNN and SVM were employed to evaluate the efficacy of these features.

Results revealed a marked enhancement in recognition accuracy when integrating both designed and learned features. This suggests that they might encapsulate complementary musical information, paving the way for future studies to delve deeper into their inherent attributes. The observed synergy between designed and learned features suggests that their combined use might be pivotal for enhanced music instrument recognition. Furthermore, the introduction of a novel dataset sourced from YouTube emphasizes the potential of incorporating real-world, diverse data in music instrument recognition tasks, setting the stage for future research in this domain.

\textbf{Keywords:} Music Instrument Recognition, Scattering Transform, MusicNN, Feature Extraction, Machine Learning, ESSID Dataset
